# HNET — Holographic Network Field Theory

Current as of October 6, 2026

## 1. What HNET is, and what it is not

HNET is a conceptual framework about what reality is made of. It was developed by one person, David, who is its sole author. The ideas are David's. AI tools helped with the wording and with checking the ideas against established physics, but did not supply them.

HNET currently has no mathematics. It does not yet produce any prediction that differs from standard physics. The most honest description is: a proposed underlying picture that reinterprets several established physical ideas as expressions of one relational substrate. It is not a new physical theory, it has not been confirmed, and it should never be presented that way.

The standard to hold throughout: "compatible with existing physics" is not the same as "confirmed by existing physics." Most of HNET's content is a reinterpretation, and an interpretation that fits known facts has not thereby been shown to be correct.

## 2. How to talk about HNET claims: three tiers

Every HNET statement falls into one of three tiers.

1. Established physics. Example: photons follow null trajectories and have zero proper time. Example: about 99% of a proton's mass comes from the energy of the strong-force dynamics binding its quarks, not from the quarks' own masses.

2. HNET's interpretation of established physics. Example: HNET reads zero proper time as meaning nothing is locally maintained for a photon between its interaction events. Relativity motivates this but does not imply it.

3. HNET's own postulates and hypotheses. Example: the massless/massive split is a true binary and a postulate of the substrate. Example: gravity as patterns settling toward increasing relational connectivity.

An unresolved question in physics gives HNET room to propose an answer. It does not give that answer any evidential weight. Never write "physics hasn't settled this, therefore HNET is supported."

## 3. The core axiom

Reality is a dynamic, self-referential substrate of distributed relational information, restructured by events.

Everything else in the framework follows from this one sentence.

## 4. The substrate

The substrate is its own kind of thing. It is not a hologram, a network, or a field, in the same way a liquid is its own kind of thing and not just a mix of solid and gas. It has all three kinds of property, more visible at different scales:

* Holographic-like at the level of the whole. Every local region encodes relational information about the entire substrate through its connections. This is not because of an external boundary. The substrate is one fully interconnected, self-referential whole. Information is distributed and redundantly encoded, though not uniformly or losslessly everywhere. It is not a copy of the whole in each region; it is a reflection of how that region relates to everything else.

* Network-like at small scales. Discrete relationships, local connections.

* Field-like at large scales. Smooth propagation, continuity, wave behavior.

Why these three, specifically. Network-like behavior was chosen first because it is the only candidate foundation with support at both the quantum scale (Bell tests) and the cosmic scale (the filament structure of galaxy distribution, the "cosmic web"). Network alone cannot produce everything the substrate must do, so field-like behavior is included for smooth, continuous propagation. Holography was added so the substrate needs no external boundary to project from. A standard boundary-projected hologram implies an outside surface the universe depends on. HNET instead treats the "surface" as just another internal dimension, giving a self-contained, boundary-less hologram. Standard holographic language that depends on an external boundary does not apply to HNET.

Space is not an empty container. It is the connected relational organization of the substrate itself. There is no outside.

Information is physical. It is not a description layered on top of some separate physical substance. A pattern is nothing more than its relational connections, with no hidden stuff underneath and no separate properties sitting apart from it. Identity, structure, and properties are all the same thing, described from different angles. There is one thing, not two things joined together. (Careful: words like "intertwined," "produces," or "creates" imply two separate things and are wrong here.)

Established vs. interpretive. "Information is physical" is compatible with quantum information and thermodynamics, for example Landauer's principle. HNET's stronger claim, that reality literally is information, is philosophical and not established.

## 5. Events, restructuring, and time

An event is an irreversible change in the substrate. Something happened, and that happening is what time passing actually is. The irreversibility is what makes it an event at all. Energy is a property an event has (how strongly it restructures), not a separate ingredient alongside time.

An event restructures the substrate locally, where it happens. It is a change in the relationships themselves, not a geometric shape bending. This applies to massless events too: a photon's restructuring is real at every interaction event, including gravitational effects, even though nothing persists locally between events.

Irreversibility and causal safety. A real event's restructuring has two parts, kept separate:

* Physical effects (changes in constraint, available paths, propagation) spread outward through the substrate's connections, bound by normal propagation limits. This is the only part ever detectable.

* The permanent fact. Separately, the event permanently changes the substrate: an unrewritable fact, fixed in causal order, with everything reachable from it now fixed as coming after it. No shared universal moment is required, only permanent ordering. This part carries no detectable content, so it can never signal faster than the connectivity-limited physical effects allow. That is what keeps HNET consistent with relativistic causality. The past is unreachable because you cannot reverse locally what was never stored locally.

Patterns. The patterns produced by restructuring are what we call matter and forces. They are the restructuring itself seen at different scales, not separate ingredients. A pattern becomes stable when its restructuring fits well with surrounding structure and can stay coordinated without forcing it to change. Patterns that don't fit reorganize or fall apart.

Local rate (time dilation). A pattern's own experienced time (proper time) depends on how deeply embedded it is. More embedding means more connections to maintain, leaving less freedom to take part in new events. That reduced participation rate is time dilation. Massless patterns have zero proper time between interaction events.

Before resolution. Before an event resolves (superposition), nothing has happened yet for that pattern. Resolution is what advances its local time.

Time is not a background dimension. In HNET, time is the irreversible ordering produced by events. This commits HNET to a "process" view of time (things become) over a "block universe" view (past, present, and future all equally exist). Relativity's four-dimensional math does not force either view. Be aware of one known pressure point: relativity of simultaneity, since there is no universal "now" for a view where only the present is real. HNET's wording ("no shared universal moment required, just permanent ordering") already avoids needing a universal now, but the core document does not yet state that this is a deliberate interpretive choice. See Section 15.

## 6. Two modes of existence

Everything in the substrate is one of two things.

Massless. Restructuring happens only at discrete interaction events: emission, absorption, scattering, and so on. Zero proper time elapses along the massless trajectory. That part follows directly from relativity. HNET further postulates that nothing is locally maintained between those events. That is HNET's own interpretation of the massless mode, not something zero proper time by itself establishes. Photons are the primary example.

Massive. Restructuring is ongoing. Patterns persist, connections are sustained, and proper time accumulates.

The binary. These are two distinct modes, not points on a spectrum. Whether a pattern enters the massive mode at all is not a matter of degree. This is a postulate of HNET's own substrate behavior, distinct from the Standard Model's Higgs mechanism, which is continuous. HNET's binary is not contradicted by the Higgs mechanism, but physics also provides no evidence for it. David's own summary cites neutrinos as a test case for how sharp the boundary is; that argument is not yet written out.

Embedding depth. Inside the massive mode, there is a separate, genuinely continuous spectrum: embedding depth, meaning how deeply embedded a pattern is. More deeply embedded means more massive. This spectrum exists only within the massive mode.

A massless pattern is not "almost massive." It is not a low point on the embedding-depth spectrum. It was never on that spectrum at all. Do not use "borderline," "partially connected," "less connected," or any sliding-scale language for the massless/massive distinction. Do not call a photon "disconnected" either. A massless pattern simply is the substrate's own restructuring at its interaction events, with nothing separate to connect. Any sense of continuity between its events belongs entirely to the observer watching, not to the pattern.

Why the binary exists is an open problem, treated as foundational, the same way the substrate's existence is a starting axiom.

The agreed analogy (nod and conversation). Imagine the substrate as people exchanging information with each other. A photon is like a nod: a complete, self-contained exchange. It is not a tiny conversation; it is a different kind of thing. A photon is a single massless pattern whose existence consists of its interaction events, like one nod-exchange with two moments to it, not two separate nods, with nothing maintained between them. Mass is like a conversation: an actively continued information exchange, built on sustained, ongoing exchange. A long conversation is not "a lot of nods," and a nod is not "a brief conversation." They are not the same kind of thing at different amounts. They are two different kinds of relationship to the same underlying activity, information exchange. This analogy is only about mass versus massless. It deliberately says nothing about duration, timing, or how things end. Earlier analogies using a screen, sparks and wood, or flashes all failed because they imported a hidden persisting backdrop or implied a spectrum.

## 7. Asymmetry

A recurring principle: symmetric states are unstable, not forbidden. A perfectly uniform or symmetric state cannot determine or drive a specific outcome, but it is not a stable resting point either. Any event breaks it into one of many possible asymmetric outcomes, with the specific outcome undetermined by the symmetric state itself. This has the same shape as spontaneous symmetry breaking in real physics, including the Higgs mechanism.

Earlier versions claimed a symmetric state is an impossible starting point. That was corrected: it is unstable, not impossible.

The principle shows up in the Big Bang's differentiation, in CP violation, and (as an unconfirmed candidate) in the abruptness of entry into the massive mode. A general supporting idea: flow of any kind requires a difference between two things, so uniform sameness leaves nothing to drive change.

## 8. Quantum behavior

Superposition is multiple possible next events being continuously attempted at once. They are not sitting passively waiting to be chosen; they are constantly pressing to occur. Because information is physical, those possibilities are physically present at once, not mere descriptions of one hidden real option.

Three stages, kept distinct:

1. Filtering. Candidates that would violate conservation laws or causal order are blocked outright. Others cancel against each other. Most non-survivors are driven to zero, not merely disfavored. Filtering runs entirely on the raw, unweighted candidates. (Each candidate carries a size and a direction, and the direction is what lets candidates reinforce or cancel.)

2. Weighting. Each survivor gets its own individual probability. In standard quantum mechanics, this is where an amplitude is squared. This is a separate step from filtering, not part of how filtering decides who survives. Several survivors can remain, each weighted.

3. Resolution. One specific survivor, chosen according to those weights, becomes the actual event. David's summary identifies this with what standard QM calls wave function collapse.

Terminology warning. The core document calls the middle stage "weighting." Earlier versions called it "squaring." David has objected to vocabulary drift and to treating "weighting" as just another word for squaring. Use the core document's stage names, and say "squaring" only when you mean the standard-QM operation. Also, "resolution" is easy to slide between senses; keep it for this quantum stage and say so if you mean anything else.

Limits. The mechanism filters between existing possibilities, so it presupposes events and rules already operating. It cannot explain the very first event (it was tested against the origin question and found circular). It also does not derive the Born rule's squaring step.

One classical-limit note. At large scales, so many paths cancel that one survives strongly, giving a classical trajectory. At small scales several survive nearly equally, giving superposition and interference. This matches the real path-integral picture. The filtering story is a qualitative shape, not a calculation.

Entanglement is two regions that interacted previously sharing relational structure; they were never fully separate. Nonlocality is correlated behavior within one connected substrate, not signaling across distance.

Decoherence as dilution. A poorly connected pattern's attempted possibilities stay distinguishable because little pulls them into the wider substrate. A pattern connected into a large surrounding structure drags correlations outward with every interaction, until its possibilities are so thoroughly correlated with the rest of the substrate that they are practically unrecoverable from the pattern alone. This is dilution, not destruction, and it does not claim the alternatives persist elsewhere (this is not many-worlds). It depends on connectivity, not mass alone. A heavy, well-isolated pattern can stay in superposition; a light, densely connected one decoheres fast. Decoherence is not the same as collapse: it explains why interference disappears, not which outcome occurs.

Open item: what "connectivity" means. In real decoherence physics, connectivity means active, local, causal coupling to a pattern's immediate environment (for example, the lattice an electron sits in). But HNET's Substrate section separately says every local region is connected to the entire substrate through holographic encoding, which is a global claim. HNET uses "connectivity" both ways and has not said whether they are one notion at different scales or two distinct notions. Candidate directions: the local-curvature versus global-topology split in general relativity, and renormalization-group flow, as models for how local and large-scale descriptions of one structure can relate.

Gravity-induced collapse (candidate). If mass is relational structure, a massive pattern in superposition is two competing relational configurations at once, a symmetric tied state, which the Asymmetry principle says cannot persist. This parallels Penrose's gravitationally induced collapse, which predicts heavier and more separated superpositions resolve faster. Real physics treats gravity-induced collapse and environmental decoherence as separate, distinguishable mechanisms. The honest reading is that they may be two mechanisms each satisfying the same requirement, not one shared mechanism. Not yet checked against HNET's own versions.

The Born rule (probability equals amplitude squared) is underived in standard QM and not derived in HNET. A real result, Gleason's theorem, shows squaring is the only consistent way to assign probabilities given three properties already built into standard QM's math. That moves the question to whether the substrate produces them:

* Conserved total probability: a candidate argument exists (a closed substrate has nowhere for a total quantity to leak). Needs a specific quantity and a tight argument.

* Non-contextuality: needs a definition of which possible events are "compatible." Candidate: two events are compatible when their attempted paths don't structurally interfere during filtering. Untested against the real technical requirement (Kochen-Specker).

* Linearity: needs a formal space where possible events can be combined and scaled. HNET has none. This is a pure mathematics gap. David's current working summary also notes that squaring may connect to the amplitude's two-part (complex) structure, and that attempts with more-part numbers (quaternions) work less well. That idea is not in the core document.

## 9. Gravity (candidate direction, not a mechanism)

No mathematics exists. Treat everything here as a direction to examine.

* Mass is relational/binding structure, not something at a point. Supported by established physics in two regimes: a proton's mass is roughly 99% strong-force binding energy; gravitationally bound systems weigh slightly less than the sum of their parts because binding energy is negative. (The sign differs by system; the shared principle is that mass lives in binding structure, not in located parts.)

* Connectivity-distance. Distance may be better understood as how much substrate connection must be traveled through, rather than raw physical separation. The two diverge only where connections are not uniformly dense.

* Falloff. Whatever plays the role of a conserved quantity spreads outward over this connectivity-based area. For ordinary evenly connected matter, this recovers the familiar inverse-square falloff. Whether it behaves differently where connections are uneven (galactic scale) is open. Flat galactic rotation curves require g(r) proportional to 1/r instead of 1/r^2; that is a concrete target, but the original motivation for connectivity-distance has not been re-checked under the current definition.

* Motion follows filtering. Every possible path is continuously attempted; most cancel or are blocked; what survives dominates. Asymmetry decides which direction filtering favors: a uniform state cannot drive anything, so motion favors increasing overall relational connectivity, the same direction entropy already increases in. "Attraction" is patterns settling into that direction, not a separate force layered on top. This fits a real, counterintuitive fact: gravitationally clumped systems have higher entropy than spread-out ones.

* At a pattern's own center, shell-theorem physics applies: net force cancels to zero by symmetry while density and pressure are at their maximum. Zero net force and maximum density are two different measurements peaking at the same place.

* Known challenges shared with entropic gravity generally: temperature and entropy are not derived from anything deeper; whether the conditions for a genuine entropic force hold is untested; a nonzero background activity level raises a question about a privileged reference frame, in tension with relativity.

* The time-dilation constraint. If HNET claims general relativity emerges as a large-scale limit, the mapping from embedding depth to time rate is not free. In the weak-field limit it must reproduce the standard result, roughly dtau/dt = 1 - GM/(r c^2). That is a standard-physics target, not an HNET result.

## 10. The Big Bang, dark energy, and origin

* The Big Bang is not an explosion inside pre-existing space and not a singularity. It is the rapid emergence and differentiation of relational structure from an early, highly constrained state. That state did not need to already be asymmetric; a symmetric start is unstable, and any event breaks it into one of many asymmetric outcomes.

* Dark energy. HNET proposes that some apparent accelerating expansion may come from differential time rates across regions of different embedding depth (deeply embedded regions run slower, voids faster). This is compatible with timescape cosmology. Timescape is a minority view; standard LambdaCDM fits supernova, BAO, and CMB data well. This is not presented as an established replacement.

* Origin of the substrate is open. Using filtering to explain the founding event was tried and found circular. A purely philosophical intuition, not derived: nonexistence is not a stable option, and existence and the first symmetry-breaking event are one instance, not two sequential facts. A "bursts" extension (each universe winds down to heat death and a new, asymmetric universe begins, with no continuity between them, and no way to ever know whether this is the first) is held as speculative intuition only. It lives in the history document, not as a settled position.

* CP violation. A mirrored (antimatter) pattern is proposed as a structural inverse, not a time-reversed one. A perfectly exact inverse would itself be forbidden pure symmetry, so mirroring cannot be perfectly exact. That small unavoidable deviation is proposed as CP violation. This explains why some imbalance must exist. It does not predict which side wins or the measured size.

## 11. Candidate extensions (all speculative)

These apply HNET's terms to other domains. None currently makes a prediction that differs from the standard account in its field. That is the bar future work must clear. Keep them separate from the physics sections.

* Life. An ordinary stable pattern persists passively by fitting its surroundings (a rock). A living pattern is a massive-mode pattern that must keep winning its own filtering to remain itself, continually re-selecting restructurings that preserve its coordinated form against the pull toward dissolution. A star holding equilibrium and a cell maintaining metabolism are the same structural kind of thing at different scales. A virus outside a host is passive (closer to the rock); inside a host it wins filtering only by borrowing the host's machinery; HNET would call it borderline, which matches biology's own long argument.

* Consciousness (candidate). The massless mode has nothing maintained between events, so nothing can be "at stake" for it; candidates must be in the massive mode. A consciousness-candidate would be a massive, densely connected, coherence-preserving pattern where a significant share of what is filtered, weighted, and resolved at each event is information about its own prior resolved states, not only external interaction. Degree of mind-likeness would be how much of a pattern's filtering takes its own accumulated history as input. This is a structural claim about access (information held and used internally), not about whether anything is felt.

* Sentience (candidate, further restriction). The above, plus asymmetric favoring of resolutions that preserve the pattern's continued coherence.

* What none of this resolves: whether any of it is felt. The hard problem remains open. Carbon is a useful reminder that arrangement, not substance, produces properties, but it only explains physical properties, not experience.

* Landauer's principle. Erasing a bit has a minimum thermodynamic cost because erasure is irreversible. HNET reads erasure as an ordinary irreversible event. The principle is independently confirmed; the open question is whether HNET predicts anything different.

* Engineered decoherence. Quantum computing's effort to isolate qubits from their environment is an ongoing experiment in the variable HNET says controls decoherence. HNET is compatible with the results but not yet distinguished from the standard account.

* Phase transitions and superconductivity. Asymmetry is the same mechanism as spontaneous symmetry breaking in materials physics. Superconductivity gives a second coherence case: electrons form one coherent state across a whole material. This is consistent with HNET once "connectivity" is read as connectivity to the immediate, causally relevant environment (cooling reduces lattice vibration), which links straight back to the open connectivity definition.

## 12. How HNET relates to existing physics

Existing equations are not wrong; they describe the substrate behaving in specific regimes. General relativity is the large-scale description of this restructuring, and quantum mechanics is the small-scale description of unresolved possibilities resolving. HNET does not replace them; it proposes a deeper picture they might emerge from. It must eventually reproduce their quantitative results.

Related existing frameworks: causal set theory (time as event ordering), relational quantum mechanics (states defined by relations), entropic/emergent gravity (Verlinde), timescape cosmology, loop quantum gravity, tensor networks, and MOND. HNET differs from each in its underlying ontology; it does not claim to absorb them.

An independent outside audit of HNET against established physics, which David reviewed, sorted it roughly as follows:

* Closely tied to established physics: zero proper time for massless particles; massless and massive paths are structurally different in relativity; mass can arise from binding energy; information has thermodynamic consequences; symmetry breaking is real.

* Reasonable but speculative interpretation: discrete-event massless existence; continuously maintained massive structure; embedding depth; space as connectivity; gravity as emergent; time as event ordering.

* Currently unsupported HNET-specific claims: the binary as a fundamental substrate property; no maintained existence between photon interactions; embedding depth generating mass and time dilation; connectivity producing the inverse-square law; gravity as entropy-driven connectivity; filtering producing quantum mechanics; the Born rule following from resolution; CP violation from imperfect mirroring; the substrate explaining the universe's origin; consciousness following from massive-mode architecture.

The audit's key correction, which David accepted: zero proper time does not by itself establish that nothing physically happens between emission and absorption. That extra step is HNET's own hypothesis.

## 13. Status

Settled, conceptually (inside HNET): time dilation, proper time, inertia, the speed of light as a ceiling, photon zero proper time, a structural arrow of time, entropy, wave/particle duality, superposition, measurement as an event with no observer role, entanglement, decoherence as dilution, information as physical, symmetric states as unstable rather than forbidden, the Big Bang as asymmetric differentiation, the massless/massive binary as a postulate, and irreversibility's compatibility with relativistic causality. "Settled conceptually" means internally coherent, not confirmed.

Promising, not settled: gravity as an entropy-favored consequence of relational structure, connectivity, filtering, and asymmetry combined.

Genuinely open and empirically exposed: dark energy via timescape.

Needs real quantitative formulas: gravity's distance dependence and rotation curves; the embedding-depth-to-time-rate function with its weak-field target; holography's precision (mapping onto existing results such as AdS/CFT and Ryu-Takayanagi); the Born rule's linearity; the specific conserved quantity behind conservation laws.

Needs tighter logical rigor, not necessarily numbers: the Born rule's non-contextuality (a definition of "compatible" events) and the definition of connectivity.

* Mathematical formalization: the largest gap overall, and the root of most others.

* Fine-tuning and the values of constants.

* Specific particle masses and force strengths. A candidate direction: several resolutions elsewhere in the framework located the real content in surrounding relational structure rather than at a point. A second angle: gravity and electromagnetism are long-range and field-like, while the strong and weak forces are short-range and network-like, matching HNET's scale-dependent properties. No numbers yet.

* Conservation laws. Candidate: whatever survives the founding symmetry break may leave a residual symmetry that enforces a conserved quantity (as electric charge stays conserved after electroweak symmetry breaks). Naming the quantity requires math.

* CP violation's size and direction.

* The Born rule.

* Why the massless/massive binary exists. Two candidates, neither confirmed: it may be a case of spontaneous symmetry breaking (which is characteristically abrupt), or its exactness may dissolve the way other apparent sharp points did.

* Gravity as an emergent entropic force (see Section 9).

* Black holes: pulled from the framework pending the gravity rework; not re-derived.

* The definition of connectivity.

* Dark matter and the vacuum: deferred, see Section 15.

## 15. Superseded, deferred, and discussed-but-not-adopted

Superseded: do not reintroduce.

* Halo gravity (a quiet center with a surrounding "halo" effect, scale-invariant, with a density floor and a Planck-star bounce replacing singularities, and a black hole information-paradox resolution built on it). Replaced by the connectivity-distance / filtering / asymmetry account in Section 9. The mass-as-relational-structure evidence was kept; the halo mechanism was dropped.

* Earlier names and framings from HNET's origin (photon event-time, higgs mass-time, correlated space, "thermodynamic process") are part of the early history only. They are not components of the current framework.

Deferred, not rejected.

* Dark matter (previously explained as the natural extent of the halo) and the vacuum (previously a dedicated explanation) both depended on halo gravity. They were set aside until gravity is rebuilt. Do not restore the old explanations; they need new accounts built on the current gravity direction.

Discussed but not adopted into the framework (do not treat as HNET claims).

* That time may or may not be a fourth dimension, and that HNET's event-based time implies a process view of time with relativity of simultaneity as its known pressure point. A caveat to the core document was offered but not confirmed.

* A thought from David that space may not exist as a neutral container at all: when something is there, what exists is its connections and their strengths, and "empty space" is just an absence. This is a sharpened version of the existing "space is relational organization" claim. Not filed.

* That dimension might be emergent, and a proposal assigning 1D/2D/3D to substrate/quantum/gravity. The specific assignment did not hold up and was dropped. Dimension and degrees of freedom are different things, and no HNET claim rests on this.

* Discussion of AI consciousness and recent interpretability research. This is general discussion, not an HNET position.

## 16. Simulator status

A toy simulator now exists in the simulator folder (hnet_sim.py, started October 6, 2026), with a report on its first runs. It is a stress test of the concepts, not validation of HNET. ## 17. Glossary (plain language)

* Substrate: the one connected relational thing everything is made of. Not a container.

* Pattern: a stable arrangement of relational connections. Its properties and identity are the same thing as the arrangement.

* Event: an irreversible restructuring of the substrate. What time is made of.

* Restructuring: a change in the relationships themselves, not a shape bending.

* Massless / massive: the two modes. Massless exists only at interaction events. Massive persists continuously. A binary, not a scale.

* Embedding depth: how deeply embedded a massive pattern is. Continuous, but only within the massive mode.

* Proper time: a pattern's own experienced time.

* Filtering / weighting / resolution: the three stages that decide which possible event becomes real.

* Decoherence as dilution: possibilities getting spread into the surroundings until unrecoverable. Depends on connectivity.

* Connectivity: how connected a pattern is to its surroundings. Its precise meaning (local/causal vs. global/holographic) is unresolved.

* Connectivity-distance: distance measured by how much substrate connection must be traveled through.

* Asymmetry: symmetric states are unstable and break into one of many outcomes.

* Irreversibility / permanent record: the unrewritable fact an event leaves in the causal ordering, carrying no detectable content.

* Relational/binding structure: the connections holding a system's parts together. HNET locates mass here, not in located parts.

## 18. Common misunderstandings to avoid

1. The substrate is not "a hologram plus a network plus a field." It is its own thing with those three properties.

2. There is no external boundary or outside. Holography here needs no outside surface.

3. A photon is not a low-mass particle, not "borderline," and not "less connected." It is a different mode entirely.

4. "Nothing maintained between a photon's events" is HNET's postulate, not a consequence of relativity.

5. Embedding depth does not apply to massless patterns.

6. Information is not a description of physical stuff; it is the physical stuff. Pattern and properties are one thing, not two things connected.

7. Filtering and weighting are separate stages. Filtering runs on unweighted candidates. Do not collapse them, and do not treat "weighting" as just a synonym for squaring.

8. Decoherence is not collapse, and it is not many-worlds persistence.

9. An event's permanent record cannot be used to signal faster than light, because it carries no detectable content.

10. Time is not a background dimension in HNET.

11. In the current gravity candidate, "attraction" is not a separate force. And gravity has no halo mechanism anymore.

12. A symmetric state is unstable, not impossible.

13. HNET is not a new physical theory yet. "Compatible with" never means "confirmed by."

14. The candidate extensions on life, consciousness, and sentience concern structure and access. They do not claim to explain whether anything is felt.

15. Dark matter and the vacuum are deferred, not answered.
